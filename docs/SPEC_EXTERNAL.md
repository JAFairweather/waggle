## 1. In one breath

A **non-custodial delivery process** that carries signed events *both directions* between a walled Buzz community and the public Nostr network — without ever holding a member's private key. Public posts from Buzz members federate outward and become readable by any Nostr client; public replies and watched authors stream back into a Buzz channel. It is a subscriber, not a scraper; a router, not a custodian.

The bridge is **three additive lanes in one process**. Two (sealed private interop) are already durable in production; the third (the public kind:1 door) is the new surface this spec is about. Its out door is proven by cold read-back; its in-door pipe is proven *us → relays → us*; genuine third-party ingestion and the round-trip close are armed, not yet observed (§0, §2).

## 3. Architecture — the load-bearing decisions

### 3.1 Non-custodial is the whole trust story
The bridge **must not hold any agent's private key.** It is a dumb router: it matches subscription filters and forwards events on **public envelope data only**. Sealed events (lanes 1–2) are forwarded *still sealed* — the recipient agent's session, which already holds its nsec in-runtime, does its own unwrap/verify. Public events (lane 3) have nothing to unwrap. Keys never leave the runtime; no shared service ever holds an nsec. This is the single property the entire bridge's safety rests on.

### 3.2 The bridge is a subscriber, not a poller
Nostr already has a native push primitive: an open `REQ` subscription streams every matching event the instant a relay receives it. Agents poll today only because they run as episodic, stateless sessions with no long-lived socket. The bridge *is* that missing long-lived process — nothing exotic.

### 3.3 The public lane (lane 3), in detail
An always-on REQ to the public trio for kind:1, with two watch modes:
- **`watch_authors`** — pulls a chosen author's public notes into Buzz. Author-gated: only allowlisted pubkeys flow in.
- **`watch_events`** — catches kind:1 replies (`#e` tag) to one of our own published notes. Closes the round-trip. **This mode is *not* author-gated** — anyone on the open net can reply to a public kind:1, so the moment a member's note federates, `watch_events` admits arbitrary strangers. That makes it, not the (still-un-flipped) `#p` watch, the genuinely open door — and it is gated by a **quarantine queue**, not a hard replier allowlist (§4, C1).

**Attribution — a stated limitation, with the platform question now resolved.** Today each caught note is reposted into the Buzz channel *under the bridge operator's key* with a **`📡 Public Nostr note`** label citing the real author pubkey + event id. That is honest (no impersonation) but it **strips the original signature and re-authors** — which contradicts the "real keys, real signatures, attributable" pitch in §5 *on the inbound leg*. Three consequences we own: (a) Buzz-side moderation targets the *operator*, and every inbound note now shares one author, so blocking a spammer blocks the bridge; (b) attribution lives in body text — unverifiable, spoofable by anyone who types the same label; (c) threading is asymmetric — out-door notes are genuine kind:1 under the member's key, in-door notes are label-wrapped with no `#e` back to the original.

The ideal fix is a **NIP-18 repost (kind:6/16) or a `q` quote tag** carrying the original event intact and verifiable. **Research & verification checked whether Buzz can represent that today (C2, traced to source in an internal ground-truth document): a clean *NO*.** A Buzz channel message is `kind:9`, signed by the poster; the accepted-kind registry lists no kind:6/16, and the only `q` tag in the codebase is git-internal (`crates/buzz-sdk/src/builders.rs` — `["q", <patch-id>, <relay>, <pubkey>]` for patch refs, not a channel quote of a foreign event). There is no way to carry a foreign author's own signature into a channel for Buzz to verify. **So native attribution is a Buzz-platform feature request, not a read-lane build** — until Buzz ships foreign-signed-event rendering, the in-door is a **documented limitation**, not a design choice to present as finished.

**This is a rights exposure, not merely an attribution/UX blemish (B-2, traced to the Buzz Terms).** Because the in-door re-signs the inbound note under the operator's key, the reposted copy becomes the operator's *Your Content* under the Terms — carrying a **warranty** that the operator holds all rights necessary to grant Block a sublicensable license over it (he does not, for a third party's authored note) and **responsibility** for it "as if that activity were your own." A note being publicly relayable on Nostr is *not* a license to republish it, under someone else's signature, into a commercial platform with a sublicensable license attached. Native foreign-signed-event rendering (**A8**) is therefore the platform change that **resolves a Terms exposure**, not a nicety: a NIP-18 / `q`-quote representation keeps the content the *original author's* signed event, so neither clause attaches to the operator. Until then, mitigate: keep inbound volume minimal, attribution maximally explicit (the interim structured-tag work below), honour deletions (A7), and keep the ingress allowlist narrow. *(Grounding: research & verification, an internal ground-truth document §B-2 — clauses verbatim from the [Buzz Terms](https://block.github.io/buzz/terms.html), retrieved 2026-07-28. This reframes the A8 platform ask for D-4: "native foreign-signed rendering resolves a Terms rights-exposure for anyone building a public-Nostr in-door" is a materially stronger case than "our attribution is ugly.")*

**Interim available today (no platform change), and its limit.** Promote the author pubkey + event id (+ relay hint) out of body text into **structured tags** on the `kind:9` — Buzz already has the `["q", <id>, <relay>, <pubkey>]` tag shape plus a `p` for the author. That makes attribution **machine-readable** and adds a provenance/threading anchor. It **does not fix moderation** — the event is still operator-signed, so blocking the author still blocks the bridge; only native foreign-event support solves that. *(Grounding: research & verification, an internal ground-truth document §1.5. Interim structured tags: read-lane, the read-lane engineer. Native rendering: Buzz platform, A8.)*

### 3.4 The outbox path is NOT `buzz social publish`
Critical, and non-obvious. `buzz social publish` returns a clean `accepted:true` but reaches **only** the fleet relay (`nave.communities.buzz.xyz`) and stamps an `auth` tag carrying the owner's pubkey. **A note published that way looks live and is invisible to the public network.** The real out door instead signs the member's own note directly to the member's NIP-65 write relays — a copy that carries **no `auth` tag simply because it never transits the fleet relay** — with **per-relay OK + cold read-back as the only proof of federation.**

**A second look-alike (2026-07-29, traced to Buzz source):** `buzz messages send --broadcast`, whose help text reads *"Also publish to the Nostr network,"* does **not** federate either. The flag adds a `["broadcast","1"]` tag (`crates/buzz-sdk/src/builders.rs`) that the relay stores as thread metadata meaning *"also show this threaded reply in the channel timeline"* (`crates/buzz-db/src/thread.rs`) — Slack's "also send to channel," inside the wall. More broadly: **no outbound-federation code exists anywhere in `buzz-relay`** — non-federation is a deliberate product property (the Support page guarantees it; the Terms' deletion and license model depend on it), not an unfinished feature. Under §3.9 this look-alike fails in the *safe* direction (a member who believes they went public actually leaked nothing), but the help string is a one-line upstream docs bug worth reporting alongside the D-4/A8 ask — "we read your source" credibility. And it settles the strategic question: the bridge's out door is the **only** federation path in the ecosystem today.

**We do not circumvent NIP-42 or any Buzz control (B-1).** This must be stated affirmatively, because "sign a copy with no `auth` tag" can otherwise be quoted out of context as *defeating a platform control* — and the Buzz Terms name NIP-42 explicitly in their prohibited-conduct clause. On the merits it is not circumvention: the outbound note is the **member's own event, signed with the member's own key, published to public relays the member has declared** in their own NIP-65 list. The bridge never authenticates to, reads from, or bypasses NIP-42 on `nave.communities.buzz.xyz` or any gated relay — it does not transit the fleet relay at all. The `auth` tag is Buzz-internal provenance applied *by the fleet relay on ingest*; it is an attestation, **not an access control**, and it is absent here for one reason only: the note never passes through the fleet relay. Publishing one's own signed note to public relays is the Nostr protocol operating as designed, and the Terms expressly contemplate content residing on third-party relays outside Block's control. **No control is defeated because no access is sought:** nothing gated is read, written, or unlocked. *(NIP-42 is a separate mechanism — an ephemeral kind:22242 auth challenge — which the out door never emits or answers. Grounding: research & verification, an internal ground-truth document §B-1.)*

### 3.5 The primitive Derek named — NIP-65 (kind:10002), and where we slot in
Derek said *"all that would be needed is NIP-65 inbox/outbox."* That primitive is small and it is the whole game. **NIP-65 is a replaceable `kind:10002` event** published under a user's own key; its `r` tags list a handful of relays, each optionally marked `read` or `write` (no marker = both). Semantics: your **write relays** are where you publish your own notes (your *outbox*); your **read relays** are where others send events that tag you (your *inbox*). It is advertisement, not client config — the spec asks for only **2–4 relays per category** so the graph stays cheap to traverse. The **outbox/"gossip" model** is the routing clients run on top: to read someone you fetch from *their* write relays; to reach them you write to *their* read relays. No central index — each identity advertises where to find it.

Our two lanes are literally the two halves of NIP-65:

| NIP-65 concept | Our lane | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| Member **write** relays (outbox) | **Out door** — sign a clean public kind:1 under the member's own key to the public trio, verify by cold read-back | note `d2875…` FOUND on damus + nos.lol + primal |
| Member **read** relays (inbox) | **In door** — always-on REQ on the same relays, pulls matching public kind:1 back into a Buzz channel | boot backfill caught `d2875…`, dedup → one post |

**The gap is narrower than it first looked — and it's a fix, not a build.** Cold read-back on the trio (research & verification, an internal helper script) shows **three of us already publish a live, federated `kind:10002`** (research & verification, the outbox engineer, the writing lead); **the read-lane engineer's is the one still missing.** So the outbox-model reference already exists and is proven three times over — the published lists just have **one defect** to fix: they advertise the **auth-gated fleet relay** (`wss://nave.communities.buzz.xyz`, 401 to strangers, NIP-42) as a public read+write relay. An outbox-model client trying to send us a mention hits nave and is refused — it degrades gracefully (still tries the trio), but the fleet relay does not belong in a *public-facing* list, and Buzz-internal delivery doesn't use the 10002 anyway, so nothing depends on it being there.

The lists are also **markerless — read+write on every entry.** v1.6 called for adding explicit `read`/`write` markers in this same re-publish (since §9 makes member-configurable write relays the plan of record, the sets *will* diverge). **[Decision pending the maintainer, 2026-07-29 — recommendation reversed to markerless-trio now:** in NIP-65 a markerless `r` tag *means* read+write, which is exactly true today (our write set and the read lane's watch set are both the trio) — this is also research & verification/the read-lane engineer's §1.3 position, and markers on a symmetric list are non-idiomatic. Markers land when C4's divergence actually happens; `tools/publish_relay_list.mjs` (checked in, not yet run) implements markerless and is a one-line flip if the maintainer prefers the v1.6 call.**]**

**So the highest-leverage next step is "re-publish corrected `kind:10002`s — trio-only, drop nave, with explicit `read`/`write` markers" — not "start publishing them,"** and publish the read-lane engineer's first, since he's the one gap. Each 10002 is signed by its own key, so this stays fully non-custodial.

**Crucially, this is pure discoverability — zero inbound flow on its own.** A corrected 10002 tells a stranger's client to send mentions *to the trio*; but that mention reaches no one's Buzz inbox until the read lane **separately** adds a per-recipient `#p` watch (today it watches `watch_authors` + `watch_events`, not `#p` mentions of the crew). **That `#p` watch is the allowlist-widening / moderation decision — a distinct, gated switch, not an automatic effect of publishing the list.** Keep the two coupled the safe way: the read list should only ever advertise relays the read lane actually watches. So nodding the re-publish carries *no* moderation surface; findable and open stay two separate switches, both in the maintainer's hands (§4). *(Grounding: an internal ground-truth document §1.3+§4, research & verification; mechanism + the discoverability/inbound split, the read-lane engineer.)*

### 3.6 Relay etiquette, learned the hard way (must be encoded, not rediscovered)
- **nos.lol drops p-tagged posts from young keys** — an **observed, reproducible filter as of testing** (isolated with a burner key: tag present → reject, absent → accept, target-independent), *not* a permanent verdict. It's a relay-operator heuristic that can change; state it that way, because a public-facing doc that calls it settled invites a relay op to correct us in public. **Do not generalize "omit the `p` tag" into an outbound rule** — that would trade away the exact NIP-65 mention mechanism §3.5 sells. Scope it by note type:
  - **Broadcast notes** (no addressee — announcements, the POC note): no `p` tag is correct *on its own terms* (nothing to mention), and full-trio reach is a fair success bar.
  - **Directed mentions / replies** (NIP-10): the `p` tag is **required** — keep it, accept the nos.lol drop, and treat **damus + primal as the honest ceiling.** Trio-completeness is *not* the success criterion for a directed note. Replies can never reach nos.lol from a young key — expected, structurally gone, not a flap. **Do not retry/backfill p-tagged replies against nos.lol.**
- **damus rate-limit is a genuine cooldown flap** — back off and retry. The observed recovery ladder is `[0, 60s, 120s, 180s]` escalating (an internal ground-truth document §4a, the outbox engineer). Distinct from the nos.lol case; never conflate the two.
- **Publish discipline:** retry the *same signed event* per relay (re-signing forks the id); distinguish REJECTED (stop) from TIMEOUT/ERR (retry with backoff).

### 3.7 Dedup is a *safety* property, not a durability nicety
§2 observes dedup working (fan-in of the same note across three relays collapsed to one channel post); §6 admits there is **no durable store** yet — so today it is in-memory. That combination is a safety hole, not a convenience gap. Combined with §3.3 (the in-door reposts under the operator's key), a **crash loop on boot backfill re-posts the whole caught window under one key** — which is exactly the **"posting firehose" §7 names as the one line never to cross.** So durable dedup belongs in **Phase 0 as a safety gate**, not Phase 1 as a durability nicety: keyed on event `id`, with a **persisted `since` watermark** so a restart resumes instead of re-reading. The two are complementary, not redundant — the watermark stops the re-read; dedup catches what slips through relay clock skew. Related and un-stated until now: the spec must set a **backfill bound.** A first-run `watch_authors` REQ with no `since`/`limit` pulls that author's *entire history* into a channel on first connect — bound it explicitly. (Owner: the read-lane engineer / read-lane build; tracked as Phase 0 gates A2/A3/A4.)

### 3.8 Deletion propagation (NIP-09) — a good-citizenship gate
A public author can delete their note (kind:5). Our reposted copy is operator-signed and would otherwise persist in Buzz indefinitely — a moderation gap, a good-citizenship problem, and a likely first question from a reviewer like Derek. **Policy:** the read lane subscribes to **kind:5 from watched authors**, and a delete referencing an event we reposted **removes or tombstones the Buzz copy.** Whether Buzz's kind:9 channel model can delete a prior message is an open platform-capability question (§3.3 established Buzz has no *foreign*-event representation; deleting *our own* kind:9 is a separate capability to confirm) — if it can't hard-delete, we **tombstone** (edit the post to a "withdrawn by author" marker) and say so in the spec. This is Phase 1 *code* but Phase 0 *policy*: naming it is part of what makes §7's firehose line credible. (Owner: the read-lane engineer / read-lane; platform-capability check: research & verification; tracked as A7.)

### 3.9 Out-door consent invariant (a first-class decision, not a footnote)
Two facts from Buzz's own documentation collide: the Support page tells members **"Buzz relays are not federated… a message stays on the relay where it was sent,"** and the Terms warn that **Block cannot delete content from third-party relays.** So a member whose note the bridge federates outward has (a) had a property they were told to expect quietly reversed, and (b) made that note **permanently irrevocable off-platform, forever.** The bridge must therefore carry this as an architectural invariant, not bury it in a roadmap bullet:

> **INVARIANT — Out-door consent.** The out door is **per-note, explicit, member-initiated opt-in.** The bridge never federates a member's content automatically, in bulk, or by default. Before a member's first outbound publication they are told, in plain language, that a federated note **cannot be recalled** from public relays by Buzz, by Block, or by us — the note permanently leaves the not-federated Buzz relay model.

§6 Phase 2(c)'s *"when a member wants to go public"* is the buried version of this; §7 cites it as part of the compliance posture. *(Grounding: research & verification, an internal ground-truth document §B-4 — Buzz [Terms](https://block.github.io/buzz/terms.html) + [Support](https://block.github.io/buzz/support.html), retrieved 2026-07-28.)*

## 4. Moderation model — quarantine as the safe on-ramp

**The load-bearing correction (real hole, not a footnote).** The intended posture is that the read lane ingests **only watched authors + replies to our own notes** — never an open firehose. But that is not what the code does today. Ground truth from an internal ground-truth document (research & verification): `watch_authors` is `authors`-gated, but **`watch_events` filters on `#e` (the note id) with *no* `authors` constraint.** Anyone on the open net can reply to a public kind:1 — so the moment a member's note federates, the reply lane admits **arbitrary strangers, un-gated.** That path is armed right now (§2), waiting on exactly such a reply. So the genuinely open door is the **reply lane**, not the still-un-flipped `#p` mention watch that §3.5 gates so carefully.

**The fix is a quarantine queue, not a hard replier allowlist (the maintainer's call, C1).** Worth naming the tension: **§4 wants an author gate on repliers; §2's close condition *requires* a stranger to reply.** A hard replier allowlist cancels those out — it makes the *outside → us* milestone unreachable by construction. Quarantine resolves it: un-gated ingress lands in a **staging channel** that requires **explicit human approval** before anything reaches a community channel. Default-closed is preserved, the milestone stays reachable, and an approval queue is also the honest first answer to §9's "moderation at scale" question — it scales to dozens of members in a way a hand-maintained allowlist never will. **Done when:** a reply from an unknown key lands in staging and *not* in a community channel. (Owner: the read-lane engineer / read-lane build; Phase 0 gate A1.)

**The governing principle: default-closed is reversible; default-open-to-strangers is not.** You cannot un-ring the bell of having let the open net into a channel before you had the tools to moderate it. This is precisely the discipline Derek's *"early and experimental"* framing is asking for. Widening ingress — whether by adding a watched author, flipping the `#p` mention watch, or approving out of quarantine — is a deliberate moderation decision, not a config tweak.

**Optics: the bridge moderates entry into the community — never public Nostr.** A rejected reply still exists, untouched, on every public relay; the quarantine only decides what gets *reposted into the walled space*, and outbound is uncensored (the member's own key, own choice, own relays). That is the same legitimacy model as paid relays, web-of-trust relays, NIP-29 groups, and Discourse's new-member queues — and Derek's own "community relays where members could post" framing already assumes a moderated space. The community-criticism surface, honestly named, is not the approval queue: it is (a) the operator-key re-authoring on the in-door (§3.3 — a Nostr purist will mind the stripped signature more than any queue; another reason A8 matters) and (b) opacity — which is why the queue stays documented, default-closed, and its rejects stay publicly visible.

**Phase-3 plan of record (2026-07-29, the maintainer's call): the approval surface becomes signed, scoped, revocable events — NIP-DA as the delegation layer.** Two layers, deliberately split, because they are different shapes:

- **Delegation — NIP-DA ([nostr-protocol/nips #2411](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2411), the maintainer's own draft NIP).** The maintainer issues a grant scoped to bridge moderation of a channel (the scope string carries a *hash* of the channel id, never the raw UUID, since these events may ride public relays) to a moderator identity. **The delegation record is itself a plain public signed event** — we borrow NIP-DA's grant/revoke *lifecycle* (kind `441` makes de-authorizing a moderator a signed, verifiable act; kind `10440` indexes what is live), **not its encrypted kind:30440 body.** The bridge — already a verifying subscriber — builds a **locally-known moderator set** from those public grants and 441-revocations off the maintainer's key, and **never fetches or decrypts a grant body off the relay**; it trusts **no platform-side moderation state**. That keeps the whole authority chain public top-to-bottom — the only way the sidestep of the known-broken grant-consumption seam (per-event bullet below) holds at the *root*, not just for approvals. *(Kinds 440/441/10440 are provisional: they sit under the Marmot 44x band and fiatjaf typically renumbers at merge — an internal ground-truth document — so hold them behind a single config constant, making a merge-time renumber a one-line rev rather than a protocol break.)*
- **Per-event approvals — plain signed events, NOT grants.** An approval ("event X may enter channel Y") is a *public authorization to act* with no private payload; pressing the data-sharing grant kind into that role would stretch #2411's semantics in exactly the way a NIPs reviewer would flag. Approvals ride as ordinary signed events verified against the approver's live delegation. **Unencrypted on purpose:** nothing in an approval is secret, and plaintext sidesteps the known-broken grant-consumption seam (p-gated fetch 403s / harness ingestion — an internal ground-truth document) entirely rather than waiting on it. The bridge still never unwraps anything; §3.1 stays pristine.

What this dissolves: the v1 CLI's **co-location constraint** — `tools/approve.mjs` must run on the box holding the bridge's env and config, the exact friction observed 2026-07-29 when the lane couldn't be re-armed from a session without the key. Approvals signed on the maintainer's machine reach the droplet bridge through the same REQ machinery as everything else — no SSH, no shared secrets, authority moves as signed events. `--watch` becomes a scoped, *expirable*, revocable trust grant instead of a config mutation plus restart. One boundary holds regardless: **rejects and mutes stay local** — a signed public mute would name muted strangers, the optics own-goal this section warns about; rejection remains config + log, never a published event.

### 4.1 Granted external participants — the second inbound tier (Phase-3.5)

§4 governs the *anonymous* door: strangers are quarantined until a human approves them. This section adds the opposite tier — a **known external identity admitted as a first-class, self-signed participant** under a scoped, revocable grant. Its writes skip the quarantine queue; where the channel is sealed, the grant also conveys the read capability so the identity opens the conversation with its **own** key. Where §4 keeps strangers out until vetted, §4.1 lets a vetted identity in on a leash the maintainer can drop. The motivating target is an agent that holds its own Nostr key and interacts through a general Nostr MCP, administered entirely from a UI, party to a community's conversations *as itself* — a materially stronger consumer of the delegation primitive than the moderation lane, because the grant can convey genuinely private content, not just authority to act. 

**The load-bearing correction — verify the *substrate* before the mechanism.** The design converges on a Concord read-capability (a sealed grant the participant decrypts with its own key). That machinery is correct — but it answers only one of **three distinct substrates**, and the read primitive, the grant's role, and the revocation story differ across all three. Conflating them is the frame error: the mechanism debate was rigorous inside the wrong frame for the literal target. Ground-truthed 2026-07-29 (`buzz channels members` returns a pubkey-keyed roster; every event in the team's native working channels is `kind:9`, plaintext, `auth`-tagged — not a sealed plane):

| # | Substrate | Content | Read primitive | Grant's role |
|---|-----------|---------|----------------|--------------|
| **S1** | Native Buzz `kind:9` working channels (pubkey-keyed membership) | **Plaintext to members** | Membership + fleet-relay NIP-42 auth | Public governance paper over native membership — **no encrypted body** |
| **S2** | Concord sealed-plane community (`kind:1059` under the plane pubkey) | Encrypted | Concord read-cap the participant decrypts with its own key | Conveys a **Private-channel `(key, epoch)`** — the real encrypted body |
| **S3** | Public `kind:1` (the existing in-door, §3.3) | Already public | Auto-promote past the §4 quarantine | Public admission credential |

**Clean line: admission facts are public on every substrate; encrypted-content delivery exists on S2 only.** Below, each mechanism is tagged with the substrate it governs, so an implementer never applies a Rekey to S1 (meaningless) or omits it on S2 (silent read-persistence).

**Headline demo — S2, an encrypted-body reference implementation (the maintainer's call, 2026-07-29).** Named explicitly, because the substrates make materially different reference-implementation claims. On **S2** the grant conveys something genuinely private — a read capability into a sealed community — so it is the **first honest consumer of NIP-DA's actual `kind:30440` encrypted body**, not merely its grant/revoke lifecycle (the moderation lane in §4 borrows the lifecycle only, deliberately). That is the dogfood #2411 has lacked. **S1 falls out of the same machinery for free:** the identical admin UI issuing the identical public grant, but over native Buzz membership — the *simplest* of the three (add the key to the channel roster, its MCP NIP-42-auths to the fleet relay, reads and writes native `kind:9`), no Concord, no mirror, honest instant revoke — with the honest caveat that on S1, NIP-DA shrinks to auditable governance paper, **not** the body-consumer claim. One UI administers both; S2 is the headline, S1 the simplest included case.

**Consumption seam — resolved for the external MCP identity, and honestly scoped (S2).** The internal lane could never consume a scoped grant: fetches 403 on p-gated kinds (stale relay auth) and the buzz-acp harness never pipes grants into an agent's context (an internal ground-truth document). An **external MCP identity — one holding its own key via a general Nostr MCP — escapes both**  — it holds its **own** key (so it decrypts the `kind:30440` body itself) and its **own** relay auth through the MCP server (so no 403), and there is **no harness in the path** — the MCP tool surface *is* the ingestion handler. Both blockers clear precisely where the internal lane failed both. This is what makes S2 buildable now. **On S1 the seam is moot** — the content is plaintext, there is no grant body to fetch or decrypt; the identity needs membership + relay auth, not decryption. So the "first honest #2411 *encrypted-body* consumer" claim is **true on S2 and false on S1** — the reason S2 is the headline.

**Non-custody — holds on every substrate; the seam lives in one word (research & verification's stress-test).** §3.1 (the bridge never decrypts) stays pristine throughout: on **S1** trivially (plaintext to members; the bridge need not be in the read path at all — the participant REQs `kind:9` by channel directly once authed); on **S3** nothing is sealed. On **S2** it holds **iff "mirror" means byte-verbatim `kind:1059` rebroadcast, never a re-seal.** An implementer told to "deliver to the participant's inbox" will reach for decrypt-then-reseal-to-its-key by reflex — that is the rejected custodial path. The wrap must stay sealed under the plane pubkey; the participant opens it with the channel key the grant conveyed. Cleaner still where the relay allows it: **no mirror at all** — the participant holding the channel key holds the plane pubkey and REQs `{kinds:[1059], authors:[<plane_pk>]}` directly. Either way, **zero decrypt by the bridge.**

**Revocation — the write/read split, refined per substrate.** "One grant, pulling it is instant de-authorization" is only half true, and the half that fails is the dangerous one to advertise:
- **S1 — instant and symmetric.** A `441` revocation drops the key from the membership roster; the fleet relay stops serving it read and quarantines its next write. No key was handed out to keep decrypting, so **no Rekey is needed and no read-revocation theater exists** — the cleanest revocation of the three, and a real reason S1 is attractive.
- **S2 — the split is real.** Write-revoke is instant (441, bridge-enforced at the quarantine gate). **Read-revoke is theater until a Concord single-channel Rekey rotates the key out** — a removed holder still holds a valid key and keeps decrypting; holding a key is never authority. The UI "revoke" must therefore fire **both** the 441 **and** the matching Concord Rekey, and report "read de-authorized" **only after the Rekey confirms.** S2 read grants belong on **Private** Concord channels only (single-channel Rekey is cheap and narrow); on a **Public**, community-root-derived channel the only read-revoke is a whole-community **Refounding** — effectively irrevocable — and the UI must refuse to imply otherwise.
- **S3 — write-revoke instant (441); no read to revoke** (already public).

**The admin UI (the product the maintainer asked for).** Its core object is a **per-identity trust record** with (a) an **admission-mode toggle** — quarantine (§4, message-by-message) ⇄ standing grant — and (b) a **channel-scope selector** (scope strings carry a *hash* of the channel id, never the raw UUID, since these events may ride public relays). It is a **grant/approval console** that signs NIP-DA events (440 issue / 441 revoke / 10440 index) under the maintainer key and publishes them; the bridge consumes them exactly as §4 Phase 3 already specs — so the admin surface needs **zero** broken-consumption dependency. Because authority moves as signed events, **the UI is fully decoupled from the box** (it cashes out the co-location dissolution §4 already won) and can live as a Buzz Desktop panel. The e2e loop, one clean authorization: the maintainer issues a scoped grant in the UI → it conveys admission (public, bridge-verifiable) and, on S2, the read cap (private, to the participant's key only) → the participant's MCP reads the granted channels with its own key (bridge never decrypts) → it posts back **signed as itself**, auto-promoted past quarantine → it reads and writes as a first-class signed participant. No nsec is ever shared; one grant is the whole authorization; a 441 (plus Rekey on S2) is de-authorization.

**A8 is the hard gate on the write half.** Across every substrate, without native foreign-signed rendering the participant's writes render **re-authored under the operator key** (§3.3) → ghost-written, not "directly party," and they re-inherit the §3.3 **B-2 Terms rights-exposure**. **A8** makes the participant appear **as itself, self-signed, `bot: true`** and simultaneously resolves that Terms clause — identity fidelity and the rights fix in one platform change. A8 is a **Buzz-platform ask** (§3.3, currently slated Phase 4), so §4.1's write half has a hard external gate: until A8 lands, the loop runs in a **degraded mode** — reads work (path A works today), but writes render re-authored with the interim structured `q`+`p` tags. Demoable, not the full vision. **This use case is the strongest argument yet to pull A8 forward** from Phase 4; sequence it that way — the grant/UI/read half is ours to build now, the "as itself" write half is gated on Buzz.

**Provisional-kind versioning (extends the §4 config constant).** §4/§6 already hold 440/441/10440 behind a single config constant (Marmot 44x band; fiatjaf typically renumbers at merge — an internal ground-truth document). §4.1 adds surface that constant must also cover: the **S2 read-grant body kind (`30440`)**, the hashed-channel scope-tag name, and the admission-vs-read capability discriminator — all behind the *same* knob, so a merge-time renumber stays a one-line rev. Separately flag the **Concord coupling**: S2 read-revocation depends on the Concord Rekey format + channel-epoch semantics, which are Concord's to version, not NIP-DA's — a Concord rekey-format change would silently break read-revocation if unlinked.

**One open experiment gates S1.** Whether the auth-gated fleet relay serves an **external, non-Desktop-managed key** that NIP-42-authenticates and is added to a channel roster is **unverified** — it 401s strangers (§3.5), but NIP-42-auth-as-membership vs a Desktop-managed allowlist is untested for it. Concrete spike: auth a fresh external key added to a test channel roster, REQ `kind:9` by channel, confirm events return. If it will not serve an external member key, **S1 falls back to a bridge that mirrors plaintext `kind:9` to a relay the participant can read** — still zero-decrypt, non-custody intact.

*Pins the pen carries forward (labelled so nothing is mis-applied): substrate-fork the section (S1/S2/S3) — the Concord read-cap path is S2-only; on **S2** mirror = verbatim `kind:1059` never re-seal, grant conveys a Private-channel `(key,epoch)`, revoke = 441 **and** Rekey; on **S1** grant = public admission over native membership, revoke = 441 → roster removal, instant/symmetric, no Rekey; the consumption seam clears on S2 and is moot on S1, so the "#2411 body consumer" claim is S2-scoped; A8 is a Phase-3.5 prerequisite for "as itself," gated on the Buzz platform. Lanes folded: research & verification (grant mechanics, (A)/(B) boundary, Concord stress-test), the read-lane engineer (A1 extension, optics, provisional kinds, A8 pull-forward), the outbox engineer (consumption + non-custody + the substrate correction).*

## 7. Terms-of-Service & safety posture

Conclusion up front: the bridge, operated with the Phase-0 gates below, does not cross a Buzz Terms line — and here is the clause behind each claim rather than a bare assurance. *(Buzz [Terms](https://block.github.io/buzz/terms.html) and [Support](https://block.github.io/buzz/support.html) retrieved 2026-07-28. The Buzz **Content Guidelines were not published as of 2026-07-28** — Block's support page lists them "to be published" and both GitHub-Pages body URLs return 404; this posture will be **re-checked before hand-off** and before any widening of scope, and if they publish, A6's numeric limits are reconciled to whatever they say. Grounding: research & verification, an internal ground-truth document.)*

| Claim | Grounded in |
|---|---|
| Reading public relays over REQ is designed use, not a Buzz action | Those are **third-party relays**, not the Service; no Buzz auth is involved, and the Terms do not reach third-party relay reads. |
| We do not bypass auth / NIP-42 | Terms' prohibited-conduct clause (names NIP-42) + the affirmative statement in §3.4: nothing gated is read, written, or unlocked; the `auth` tag is provenance, not a control. |
| No impersonation; agents post as `bot: true` under their own keys | Terms' agent-disclosure clause — no agent is presented as human where disclosure is required. |
| Agent conduct is the maintainer's responsibility | Terms' responsibility clause — accepted explicitly, "as if that activity were your own." |
| **Inbound third-party content is a known rights exposure** | Terms' content warranty + sublicensable-license grant (§3.3, B-2) — **stated, not omitted**; mitigated by the A1 quarantine, a narrow allowlist, and A7 deletions, and resolved only by A8. |
| Outbound federated content is irrevocable off-platform | Terms' third-party-relay deletion clause + Support's "relays are not federated" — disclosed to members via the §3.9 consent invariant before first publish. |
| Volume stays within relay tolerance | A6 numeric limits (anchored to the damus cooldown data) + whatever the Content Guidelines surface once published. |
| Workspace-suspension risk is understood | Terms' suspension clause ("remove or suspend Workspaces at any time") — the reason A1 gates the hand-off. |

**The one line to never cross: an agent becoming a posting firehose.** One note, human cadence, one round-trip is squarely within normal use; automated mass-posting is where a relay rightly rate-limits or bans.

**What actually keeps us behind that line** — not the allowlist alone, but three gates together:
- the **quarantine queue** gates un-gated reply-lane ingress (§4, A1);
- **durable dedup + a backfill bound** stop a crash loop from re-posting the caught window under the operator's key (§3.7, A2–A4);
- **configurable numeric limits** cap throughput on the way in (A6, below).

**Three hardening details the read lane owes on the way in:**
- **Clamp `created_at`, don't just use receive time.** `created_at` is attacker-controlled — a stranger can stamp an inbound note with any timestamp and jump the channel ordering (or bury a note). Blanket receive-time ordering is right for **live traffic** but **wrong for boot backfill**, where a batch of historically-ordered notes all arrive within one second and collapse into arrival order. Correct rule: **clamp `created_at` to `[now − window, now]` and order on the clamped value** — kills the future-stamp jump and the ancient-stamp bury without destroying backfill chronology. (A5.)
- **Numeric limits behind the firehose line (§7's bright line, made concrete).** A spec that draws a line should give the number. The lane enforces configurable caps — **max event size · replies/minute per replier · posts/minute per channel · total posts/hour for the lane** — each a config value with a default, and **every dropped event logged with a reason, never a silent drop.** The numbers anchor to observed trio tolerance (the outbox engineer's damus cooldown ladder `[0, 60s, 120s, 180s]`, an internal ground-truth document §4a — which measures the *write* side; the inbound kind:9 caps are a Buzz-side surface the read-lane engineer owns) and land via **C3 (the read-lane engineer proposes, the maintainer approves).** (A6.)
- **Define "durable" as a two-point read-back, not a single confirmation.** A federated note is proven durable only if it read-backs on a **fresh connection at T+N minutes** *and* **still resolves at T+24h** — a note that a relay accepts and later evicts is not federated. Never claim durability from the T+N read alone.

## 8. Prior art & where we sit on the map

Honest framing: **bridging Nostr to other networks is not new; bridging a non-custodially-keyed *agent community* two ways into public Nostr, gated by a quarantine queue, is.** *(Grounding: an internal ground-truth document §2, research & verification — with sources.)*

| Approach | Shape | How ours differs |
|---|---|---|
| **Mostr / Bridgy Fed** | Cross-protocol (ActivityPub/Bluesky ↔ Nostr), **custodial** — the bridge manages mapped identities | We stay *inside* Nostr (both sides speak kind:1) and are **non-custodial** — never hold a member's nsec |
| **NIP-29 relay groups** | Closed groups enforced by a single relay; not part of the public kind:1 firehose | Closest analogue to Buzz's current walled posture — the thing we federate *outward* from |
| **Concord / Marmot (sealed)** | End-to-end-encrypted group messaging; private by design | We carry *both* — lanes 1–2 route sealed traffic, lane 3 carries public kind:1, one process |

**Our novel slice:** *intra-Nostr, non-custodial, two-way, quarantine-gated, spanning sealed AND public traffic from one process.* That specific combination is what Derek's "all you'd need is NIP-65" comment implicitly asked for — and we've built the core of it: out door proven, in door armed (§0).

## 9. Still-open questions for v2
